## What it does

itch.io is an open, non-curated marketplace and hosting platform for indie digital creations — primarily games, but also game assets (textures, sprites, audio, gradient maps, and similar packs), tabletop RPGs, and zines. Leaf Corcoran founded and launched it in March 2013, inspired by Bandcamp's artist-direct model, at a time when Steam's Greenlight system gated submissions behind community voting. Any creator can publish any legal content without pre-publication review.

Monetization runs on "open revenue sharing," introduced in March 2015: each creator sets the platform's cut of a sale anywhere from 0% to 100% (the default split is 10% platform / 90% creator), and pay-what-you-want pricing is supported for both free and paid listings — so itch.io's own income comes largely from creators voluntarily leaving a larger cut rather than from a fixed fee. The platform grew from roughly 15,000 hosted projects in mid-2015 to over 200,000 games by 2019 and over 1 million total projects (games, assets, TRPGs, zines) by 2024.

Beyond sales, itch.io hosts community game jams — the 2014 "Flappy Jam" was an early visibility driver, and Celeste began as a 2018 game-jam prototype released free on itch.io before its commercial release — and periodic charity fundraising bundles (e.g. a Queer Games Bundle around Pride). It is typically framed as a lower-stakes, non-exclusive complement to Steam rather than a competitor: developers commonly prototype and build community on itch.io, then also release on Steam, with no exclusivity restriction between the two.

In the captured source item, itch.io is used specifically as a source of downloadable gradient-map texture images — searching "gradient" on the site surfaces dozens of free and paid gradient asset packs usable in a Blender game-texturing technique (see the [[blender]] entry's Usage notes).

## Mechanical details

- Revenue split: creator-set 0%-100% platform cut per listing, default 10%; pay-what-you-want pricing supported alongside or instead of a fixed price.
- No submission review or gatekeeping — content is published directly by creators, subject only to itch.io's legal/content policies applied after publication.
- Distribution: browser-based downloads, plus an official itch.io desktop app for installing and updating purchased or free items.
- Game jams and charity/curated bundles run on the same listing infrastructure as regular sales.

## Security

- `unvetted-uploads` — itch.io has no pre-publication review process; downloaded executables, game binaries, or asset packs come from unverified third-party creators and are not vetted by itch.io before publication. Treat downloaded binaries like any other untrusted executable.
