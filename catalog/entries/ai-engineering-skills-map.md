## What it says

A skills taxonomy published by Andrew Ng and DeepLearning.AI in *The Batch* (Aug 14, 2026). It names the AI-engineering skills the authors identified as most valuable, derived from an analysis of 10,000+ job postings, dozens of structured interviews with AI experts, hiring managers, and recruiters, surveys, and other online data — a process the author likens to clustering over a large dataset of jobs and interviews to surface the skills that matter now and in the near future.

It distinguishes **AI Engineering skills** (broad — held by full-stack, data, DevOps, ML, and AI engineers alike) from the **AI Engineer** role (a narrower job title), arguing all developers now need AI-engineering skills.

The four skills:

| Skill | What it covers |
|-------|----------------|
| Building and deploying AI applications | Understanding AI building blocks (LLMs, context engineering, RAG, agentic workflows, ML/DL) and using statistical techniques to measure, steer, and govern AI systems so they behave more predictably; driving disciplined evals and error-analysis loops. |
| Software engineering fundamentals | Recognizing tradeoffs between cost, scalability, reliability, speed, security, and privacy; making better stack, architecture, data-store, and testing decisions; steering coding agents using precise software-engineering language. |
| Using coding agents | Holding a mental model of how agents work, their limits and workarounds; managing agent context, trading off planning vs execution, closing loops with verifiers/evals, working with specs (and when not to), orchestrating multiple agents, avoiding pitfalls such as an agent corrupting a production database; and continually trying new tools as practices evolve. |
| Shaping the build | Product sense, business context, and customer goals; deciding what belongs in the spec rather than only implementing a supplied design; knowing when to build an MVP for user testing vs slow down and build carefully; taking on greater ownership and agency. |

The author frames a mindset of continuous learning as the foundation under all four skills.

## Key takeaways

- The central premise: AI applications differ from traditional software because their outputs are unpredictable, so the differentiating skill is using statistical and eval techniques to make behavior more predictable.
- Presented as a living document — the author said he would elaborate each skill in later letters, share a more detailed map, and keep updating it as AI evolves.
- Skill 1 ("Building and deploying AI applications") was subsequently expanded into six sub-components: LLM foundations, grounding models with data, building agentic systems, evaluation-driven development, operating in production, and machine learning foundations.
