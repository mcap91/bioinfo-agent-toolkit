## What it says

An automation builder's observation, distilled as "you cannot automate a habit." A brief for an automation ("take the order and send it to the kitchen, same as our staff do") assumes a process that is actually undocumented and inconsistent. Ask three people and you get three descriptions: the owner's four-year-old design, the manager's version with a half-dozen layered exceptions, and the operator's real workflow that involves a notebook. Even a request as simple as "the menu" resolves to no single answer — the same dish is priced differently across outlets, half of it stops being served after 4pm, and two items are the same dish under different names.

## The reusable point

- Most of the work in an automation/agent project is not the build; it is eliciting and codifying a process that exists only as habit, then getting someone with authority to sign off on the version that gets encoded.
- Projects that went badly went badly in discovery, not in the build; projects that went well were the ones where an authority sat down and made the calls.
- Practical tension raised (left open): clients resist paying for discovery because "it looks like meetings, not software" — so treat discovery as explicit, billable work rather than absorbing it into a build quote.
- Applies directly to agent builders: an agent can only execute a process that has been made explicit; encoding an inconsistent habit encodes the inconsistency.
